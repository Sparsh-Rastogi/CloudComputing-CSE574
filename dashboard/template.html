DD-HPSO: Deadline-Aware, Duplication-Enabled, HEFT-Seeded Particle Swarm Optimization for Cost and Energy Efficient Scientific Workflow Scheduling on IaaS Clouds

Sparsh Rastogi (Roll No. 24095072)  and  Nisha Mittal (Roll No. 24095071)
Department of Computer Science & Engineering, CSE-574 Cloud Computing
Under the Mentorship & Guidance of: Dr. Prasenjit Chanak (Associate Professor, CSE Dept.)

Abstract— Scientific workflow scheduling on Infrastructure-as-a-Service (IaaS) clouds poses a complex multi-objective optimization challenge where execution makespan, monetary rental cost, and energy consumption must be optimized under strict user-defined deadlines. While list heuristics such as HEFT and task-duplication strategies reduce makespan, they frequently induce severe monetary cost inflation (+15% to +45%) by leasing additional virtual machine (VM) capacity. Conversely, metaheuristics such as Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) offer global exploration but suffer from high run-to-run variability and deadline violations under tight time constraints. To address this fundamental research gap, we propose DD-HPSO, a Deadline-Aware, Duplication-Enabled, HEFT-Seeded PSO algorithm. DD-HPSO seeds the swarm with topological HEFT schedules, enforces Deb's feasibility rules without penalty parameters, distributes deadlines across task levels, and executes selective critical-parent duplication strictly constrained by zero-cost threshold (θC = 0) and a 5% energy cap (θE ≤ 0.05 Eref). Comprehensive empirical benchmarking across 21 test cases and 1,323 runs demonstrates that DD-HPSO achieves 100.0% deadline compliance, matches HEFT's makespan within 2.3%, reduces monetary cost by 8.9% and energy by 6.8% relative to HEFT/HEFT-TD, and statistically outperforms state-of-the-art 2025/2026 baselines (Wilcoxon p < 0.003).

Index Terms—Cloud Computing, Scientific Workflows, Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG), Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), Task Duplication, HEFT Seeding, Hourly Billing Periods, Energy Efficiency, Deb's Feasibility Rules.

I. Introduction & Literature Context

A. Motivation & Problem Statement

Data-intensive scientific applications in astronomy, earthquake hazard modeling, genomics, and gravitational wave detection are structured as Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs). Mapping these DAGs onto commercial IaaS clouds involves intricate multi-faceted trade-offs:

1) Cloud VMs are heterogeneous in computing capacity and hourly rental rates.
2) Cloud providers bill by discrete time intervals (e.g. 1 hour = 3600s), where fractional usage is billed in full.
3) Provisioning introduces non-negligible boot delay (β = 97s).
4) Inter-VM data dependencies introduce transmission delays that keep VMs idle, stretching rental duration and wasting energy.

A tenant-side static scheduler must produce an offline task-to-VM assignment that strictly satisfies a hard deadline D while jointly minimizing monetary cost C and dynamic/idle energy E.

B. Inspiring Literature & Combined Research Gap

Our project is inspired by three foundational works that reveal clear open gaps:

• Genez et al. (IEEE/ACM UCC 2018) [1]: Proposes HEFT-TD and Lookahead-TD, duplicating critical parent tasks to eliminate communication. Gap: It is deadline-unaware and cost-blind, raising cost by +10% to +45% on CyberShake and Epigenomics.

• Rodriguez & Buyya (IEEE TCC 2014) [2]: Applies PSO to minimize cost under hourly billing and deadlines using Deb's rules. Gap: Ignores energy and task duplication; random swarm initialization leads to high variance and frequent deadline violations on tight budgets.

• Li et al. (IEEE TSC 2018, CEAS) [3]: Employs multi-stage heuristics (sub-makespans, task merging, VM reuse, DVFS). Gap: Greedy without global search; strongly relies on DVFS hardware control which is inaccessible to public IaaS cloud tenants.

The Combined Research Gap: No existing scheduler integrates heuristic seeding (to tame swarm variability), selective task duplication (to mitigate communication latency), discrete hourly billing accounting, and tenant-side energy tracking inside a unified global search framework. DD-HPSO is designed to resolve this gap.

DAX Dependency Parsing

Each XML file defines <job> elements with execution times and <uses> tags indicating input and output filenames. Our parser matches shared filenames between parent and child jobs to compute precise data transmission volumes (dij). When explicit filename references are omitted (e.g. CyberShake zip archiving), the parent's total output volume is utilized as fallback.

Topological Ordering & Upward Ranks

Tasks are ordered based on the HEFT upward rank metric:

ranku(i) = w̄i + maxj ∈ succ(i) ( dij / B + ranku(j) ) (1)

where w̄i is the average execution time across all VM types, and B = 100 MB/s. Sorting tasks in descending order of ranku yields a guaranteed valid topological execution sequence.

V. Proposed Methodology: DD-HPSO Architecture

A. Algorithmic Workflow & Seeding

DD-HPSO resolves PSO's slow convergence and run-to-run instability through deterministic heuristic seeding:

1) HEFT Seeding: Particle 1 is initialized with the HEFT task-to-VM assignment plus coordinate center xi = vmHEFT(i) + 0.5. Particles 2..N are initialized as perturbed mutations (pmut = 0.1) of Particle 1. This guarantees the search begins from an already high-quality, feasible frontier.
2) Fixed Topological Order: To cut the search space in half, particles encode only task-to-VM mapping, and the builder decodes tasks in fixed ranku order.

B. Sub-Deadline Duplication Trigger

Rather than duplicating tasks blindly across candidate VMs, DD-HPSO computes task sub-deadlines:

SDi = AFTHEFT(i) · ( D / MHEFT ) (4)

Duplication is evaluated for task i only if its non-duplicated finish time exceeds SDi (AFTold(i) > SDi), completely avoiding wasteful compute time for tasks already ahead of schedule.

C. Strict Cost & Energy Duplication Filters

When triggered on critical parent p*, the duplicate is placed in an idle gap on VM k if and only if three joint conditions hold:

ΔC ≤ 0   (θC = 0) (5)

Condition (5) strictly prohibits duplicates from causing a VM to cross into a new unpaid 3,600s billing interval.
Extra energy must also comply with cumulative ceiling:

Σ ΔE ≤ θE · Eref   (θE = 0.05) (6)

D. Multi-Objective Evaluation & Deb's Feasibility

Solutions are normalized relative to HEFT and evaluated using Deb's feasibility rules:

min F = 1/3 (C / Cref) + 1/3 (E / Eref) + 1/3 (M / Mref),   s.t. M ≤ D (7)

VI. Results Analysis, Fallbacks & Conclusions

A. Where DD-HPSO Wins

• 100% Feasibility Under Tight Deadlines: At D = 1.2x, Plain PSO, Rodriguez PSO, and IPSO-GWO frequently violate deadlines (SR of 20%–45% on Inspiral and Epigenomics). DD-HPSO achieves 100.0% feasibility across all 21 cases.
• Makespan Compression Without Cost Penalty: Selective duplication removes inter-VM transfer stalls, outperforming HEFT-TD's cost inflation while keeping makespan within 2.3% of the absolute lowest achievable schedule.
• Statistical Superiority: Wilcoxon signed-rank tests confirm statistically significant makespan gains over all non-HEFT baselines (p < 0.003).

B. Honest Analysis of Fallbacks & Failure Modes

• Relaxed Deadlines (D = 2.0x): Under loose constraints, pure cost heuristics (WPSO, PACM, and Rodriguez PSO) downscale tasks onto cheap Small instances, achieving 20%–35% lower cost than DD-HPSO. DD-HPSO preserves faster instance types to optimize makespan.
• Low CCR Workflows: On Epigenomics (CCR = 0.05), communication is negligible compared to computation. Duplication yields no makespan reduction, though the cost filter θC = 0 safely prevents cost increases.
• Scheduling Overhead: DD-HPSO takes ~1.04s per run, compared to 0.003s for one-pass heuristics.
